UNIVERSITY OF DELHI
B.A. (Hons.) Sociology - Semester II
Paper SOC 202: Methods of Sociological Enquiry
Credits: 6 (Lectures 5, Tutorial 1)

Course Objectives: To introduce students to the logic of social research and to
build practical competence in designing studies, collecting data and analysing
it responsibly.

Unit I: The Logic of Social Research
Philosophy of social science. Positivism and interpretivism. Objectivity,
reflexivity and value neutrality. The relationship between theory and method.
Formulating a research question and a researchable problem. Literature review
and conceptual framework. Hypothesis, variables and operationalisation.

Unit II: Qualitative Research Methods
Ethnography and participant observation. Field notes and thick description.
Semi-structured and in-depth interviewing: designing an interview guide,
probing, rapport. Focus group discussions: moderation and composition. Case
study method. Oral histories and life histories. Thematic analysis: open coding,
axial coding, building a qualitative codebook and identifying themes.

Unit III: Quantitative Research Methods and Survey Design
Survey research design. Sampling: probability and non-probability methods,
simple random, stratified, cluster, purposive and snowball sampling. Sample
size and sampling error. Questionnaire construction: question wording, avoiding
leading and double-barrelled questions, Likert scales, pre-testing and piloting.
Coding and data entry. Descriptive statistics: measures of central tendency and
dispersion. Cross-tabulation and chi-square. Interpreting correlation.

Unit IV: Data Collection, Fieldwork and Ethics
Primary and secondary sources. Use of census data, NSSO and NFHS datasets.
Content analysis of documents and media. Research ethics: informed consent,
anonymity, confidentiality, and research with vulnerable groups. Institutional
review and ethical clearance. Plagiarism and research integrity.

Unit V: Analysis, Writing and Dissemination
Triangulation and mixed methods. Writing a research report: structure,
referencing and citation conventions. Presenting findings through tables and
charts. Writing a policy brief for a non-academic audience. Community needs
assessment and stakeholder mapping.

Practical Component: Students will design and pilot a short questionnaire on a
contemporary social issue, conduct five semi-structured interviews, code the
transcripts, and submit a 2000-word analytical report with a one-page policy
brief.

Suggested Readings:
1. C.R. Kothari, Research Methodology: Methods and Techniques.
2. Earl Babbie, The Practice of Social Research.
3. Alan Bryman, Social Research Methods.
