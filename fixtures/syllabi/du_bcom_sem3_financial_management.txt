UNIVERSITY OF DELHI
B.Com (Hons.) - Semester III
Paper BCH 3.2: Financial Management
Credits: 6 (Lectures 5, Tutorial 1)

Course Objectives: To familiarise students with the principles and practices of
financial management and to develop the ability to apply them to real corporate
decisions.

Unit I: Introduction to Financial Management
Nature, scope and objectives of financial management. Profit maximisation versus
wealth maximisation. Time value of money: compounding and discounting, present
value and future value of a single amount and of an annuity. Risk and return:
expected return, standard deviation, coefficient of variation.

Unit II: Capital Budgeting
The capital budgeting process. Cash flow estimation: incremental cash flows,
sunk costs, opportunity costs. Payback period and discounted payback period.
Net Present Value (NPV), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), Modified IRR,
Profitability Index. Capital rationing. Risk analysis in capital budgeting:
sensitivity analysis and scenario analysis.

Unit III: Working Capital Management, Liquidity and Cash Flow
Concepts of working capital: gross and net working capital. Operating cycle and
cash conversion cycle. Estimation of working capital requirements. Management of
cash: cash budgets and 13-week cash flow forecasting. Receivables management:
credit policy, ageing schedule, debtors turnover. Inventory management: EOQ,
reorder level, inventory turnover ratio. Ratio analysis for liquidity: current
ratio, quick ratio, debt service coverage ratio.

Unit IV: Cost of Capital and Capital Structure
Cost of equity using the dividend discount model and the Capital Asset Pricing
Model (CAPM). Estimation of beta. Cost of debt and cost of preference capital.
Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC). Capital structure theories: net income
approach, net operating income approach, Modigliani-Miller hypothesis.
Operating, financial and combined leverage. EBIT-EPS analysis.

Unit V: Dividend Decisions
Dividend policy and its determinants. Walter's model and Gordon's model. Bonus
shares and stock splits. Payout ratio and retention ratio.

Practical Component: Students are expected to prepare a 3-statement financial
model in a spreadsheet for a listed Indian company, compute its WACC, and
present a one-year cash flow projection.

Suggested Readings:
1. Khan M.Y. and Jain P.K., Financial Management, Tata McGraw Hill.
2. Pandey I.M., Financial Management, Vikas Publishing House.
3. Ross, Westerfield and Jaffe, Corporate Finance, McGraw Hill.
